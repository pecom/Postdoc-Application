\indent \indent The best model for the cosmos, $\Lambda$CDM, is being pushed and pulled as the latest results of evolving dark energy from DESI and various tensions ($H_0$ and $S_8$) test our understanding of the Universe.
Taking a look at how these parameters vary with redshift and with independent systematics can give us a sharper understanding of our Universe which is where large-scale weak lensing galaxy surveys can come in.
While there are a variety of probes that galaxy surveys can use, I am particularly excited by galaxy clusters as they can give us strong constraints on $\sigma_8(z)$, $\Omega_M(z)$, and $f_{\textrm{gas}}(z)$ which can tell us about stucture growth.
Clusters are somewhat forgiving making them ideal test cases for early data which we have an abundance of with Euclid, Roman, and Rubin coming online and starting to make data public. 


I want to use these instruments to study cluster via weak lensing to constrain the redshift evolution of cosmological parameters.
To obtain the strongest results possible, I want to reduce weak lensing systematics and make use of joint processing to maximize the data we have.
I will perform weak lensing mass measurements of clusters that can directly feed into a cluster cosmology study or act as a mass calibration for one and measure any redshift dependence of derived parameters.



\textbf{Previous and Current Research}
I have spent most of PhD working on weak lensing systematics and validating weak lensing measurements with the Vera Rubin Observatory through cluster studies.
The weak lensing systematic I have focused on is \textbf{unrecognized blends}, or objects (typically galaxies) that are mistakenly identified as only one due to severe amounts of blending (see Fig.~\ref{fig:blend_ex} for an example).

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.3\linewidth]{figs/annotated_blends2_v2.pdf}
	\caption{Example unrecognized blend in the COSMOS field with observed magnitudes and estimated photometric redshifts attached. Dark blue is the object as detected and measured by Subaru, dashed-dotted red and purple are detections and measurements from HST.}
	\label{fig:blend_ex}
\end{figure}

The repercussions of unrecognized blends via detection bias alone have been studied previously, showing shifts in cosmology parameters at the level of a few percent \cite{ben_clustering, blends_2011, kidsblends_2025}, and an early study including the impact on shape and flux measurements found up to a 2$\sigma$ shift in S$_8$ when conducing a cosmic shear analysis \cite{erfan}.
Typically, these objects are dealt with by measuring bias corrections from large expensive image simulation which are best suited for cosmic shear studies.
Cluster weak lensing suffers from larger shears and more blending limiting the applicability of this calibration technique to their studies. 
To that end, I have been studying these objects and novel mitgation strategies.
In my first PhD project I showed that such objects can be readily detected from the catalog level using machine learning techniques to assign a blend probability based on measured fluxes.
While that work showed we can remove them from the catalog, it's unclear if such a removal would ultimately improve our cosmology. 
To answer that, I investigated how this blend removal method impacts shear calibration and if this removal can be done without inducing any new biases using the AnaCal shear framework.
We showed that not only does the bias reduce, the bias can be almost totally removed with a 30-40\% sample cut which may be worthwhile when we are systematics limited.
Finally, outside of blending I have been leading efforst on some of the first weak lensing results with the Vera Rubin Observatory through studying the galaxy cluster Abell 360.
I specifically worked on the source sample selection, photo-$z$ validation with DESI, shear measurement with shapeHSM and AnaCal, and cluster mass estimation in that project.
Those experiences directly equpiped me to work on the latest Rubin dataset, data preview 2 where I am leading efforts on making weak lensing measurements around clusters using AnaCal. 
While these experiences are relatively disjoint, they have provided me with a strong base of fundamentals that I want to build on as I push towards a full cosmological study with galaxy clusters






I have been focusing on unrecognized blend as they are an unavoidable systematic for weak lensing.
Continuing to work on understanding the biases introduced by unrecognized blends in all aspects of galaxy cosmology is vital work that must be done for any ground based galaxy survey like LSST.
While challenging, I am excited to continue working on and learning about unrecognized blends along with studying galaxy clusters to study the universe and probe beyond $\Lambda$CDM cosmology.
